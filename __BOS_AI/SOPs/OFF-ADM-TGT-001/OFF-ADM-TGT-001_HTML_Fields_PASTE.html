ℹ Why This SOP Exists

Every fall and spring cleanup work order carries a target in man-hours. BOS prefills it when the work order is created from the most recent completed cleanup at the same property, and shows it to the crew above the clock buttons and on their schedule card. This SOP covers how the office checks and sets that target when scheduling, so every cleanup starts with a number the crew can see and the supervisor can measure against. The target is a yardstick, never the bill — labor is charged on the hours actually worked and marked billable.

field_sop_responsibilities — Rules & Responsibilities

Paste this HTML into
field_sop_responsibilities
Switch to Source view in the CKEditor toolbar, then paste.
MUST
  • When scheduling a cleanup, office must check Target Hours on the work order edit form (Office Administration › Billing, right after Total Time).
  • Where Target Hours is blank, office must enter a target from last year's work order, the estimate or contract section, or the supervisor's judgment — before the crew's day starts.
  • Office must adjust a prefilled target when the scope changed since last year (new beds, a bigger property, a reduced contract).
  • Office must treat the "Last time: X hrs on date" line on the work order page as the source of the prefill — if it looks wrong for the property, ask the supervisor before trusting it.
  • Supervisors must compare Clocked vs Target on completed cleanups (the sign-off line and the clock banner show it) and raise large misses with the crew lead.
MUST NOT
  • Office must not invent a target with no basis. A blank target is honest; a made-up one teaches the crew the number means nothing.
  • Office must not change the target after the crew has started the job unless the supervisor agrees — it is the yardstick for that day.
  • Office must not use the target to set hours on the invoice. Labor is billed on actual billable hours.
  • Office must not expect a prefill on a property with no completed prior-year cleanup, or where last year's record was unusable (BOS skips a prior whose hours exceed the long-shift cap per person and logs it). Those stay blank for you to set.

Overview

1. CreatedBOS prefills Target Hours from last year's completed cleanup.
2. CheckOffice reads the target when scheduling; fills blanks; adjusts for scope.
3. CrewSees Target vs Clocked above the clock buttons and on My Schedule.
4. ReviewSupervisor compares actual to target after completion.

Pre-Checks

  • The work order exists and has a property.
  • You can see last year's cleanup for the property (the "Last time" line on the work order page, or the prior work order).

Steps

1Open the work order edit formOffice
  • Office Administration › Billing. Target Hours sits right after Total Time.
  • A prefilled value came from the most recent Complete, Invoiced or Paid cleanup of the same kind at this property.
2Sanity-check a prefilled targetOffice
  • Compare it with the "Last time: X hrs on date" line on the work order page and with what you know of the property.
  • If the scope changed since last year, adjust the number and say why in the work order notes.
3Fill a blank targetOffice
  • Look up last year's work order (hours), the estimate or the contract section for the cleanup.
  • No basis anywhere? Ask the supervisor for a number, or leave it blank — never guess.
4Save and confirmOffice
  • Save the work order. On the work order page the banner above the clock buttons reads Target X hrs · Clocked 0 hrs.
  • Once scheduled, the crew's My Schedule card and the Dispatch tile show "Target X hrs".
5Review after completionSupervisor
  • The sign-off line and the clock banner show hours over or under target. Discuss large misses with the crew lead — scope, crew size, or a target that needs updating for next year.
  • Next year's prefill uses this year's actual hours, so a corrected scope now fixes the forecast later.

ℹ Why some targets are blank

When this shipped (2026-11-11), 68 of 86 open cleanups got a target. 17 had no completed prior-year cleanup at the property with hours recorded; 1 was skipped because last year's record was a single 219-hour punch from a forgotten clock-out. Blanks are for the office to set.

⚠ The target is not the bill

A crew that finishes under target is not billed less, and a crew that runs over is not billed more. Labor is charged on the hours actually worked and marked billable at sign-off.

Quality Checks

  • Every cleanup scheduled for the coming week carries a Target Hours value, or a note saying why not.
  • The crew's My Schedule cards for those days show "Target X hrs".
  • No target was changed after the crew started without the supervisor's agreement.

Completion

  • Nothing else at scheduling time.
  • Monthly in season, the supervisor reviews actual vs target by property and notes properties whose scope changed for next year.

Do / Don't

✕ Don't✓ Do
Leave a blank target because "the crew knows the place"Fill it from last year's work order or the estimate so the crew and the supervisor measure the same thing
Change the target at noon because the crew is running longLeave the yardstick; review the miss with the crew lead afterwards
Bill the target hoursBill actual billable hours — the target never touches the invoice
Trust a prefill that looks wrong for the propertyCheck "Last time" and the prior work order; ask the supervisor
